The science

Signal. Surface EMG is band-limited noise (about 20–450 Hz) whose amplitude grows with muscle activation. The hand takes its RMS over a sliding window; anything above the threshold becomes a motor command. Longer windows are calmer but lag.

Grip. Two opposing contacts hold an object only while friction can carry its weight: 2μN ≥ m(g + a). Below that it slides out. Above the object's breaking load it cracks, bursts or buckles. Lifting follows a minimum-jerk path, the smooth profile people use.

Most prosthetic hands give no sense of touch, so users grip by eye. Switch off grip feedback to feel how hard that is. Masses and friction are typical values; breaking loads are approximate.

The science

Motion. Hip, knee and ankle follow the average angles measured in gait labs for healthy adults, on a body built from standard anthropometric ratios (1.75 m, 75 kg). The white dots are the reflective markers a motion-capture system would track.

Force. The ground force isn't drawn from a template. It is Newton's second law on the model's own centre of mass, F = m(g + a), shared between the feet while both are down. The gold line is that centre of mass vaulting over each leg like an inverted pendulum.

Prosthesis. A passive knee must stay straight while it carries weight, and a carbon foot can't push off. The body vaults higher over the stiff leg and timing goes lopsided. A powered knee and ankle put much of that motion back.

The science

Kinematics. Link lengths and masses match a Universal Robots UR5. Inverse kinematics is solved in closed form: the base turns toward the target, the shoulder and elbow form a triangle solved with the law of cosines, and the wrist cancels their angles so the gripper points straight down.

Motion. Every joint follows a trapezoidal velocity profile: accelerate, cruise at the speed limit, decelerate. That's how industrial controllers move without jerking the arm.

Torque. The bars are the static torque each joint needs just to hold the arm up, τ = Σ m·g·x, against the UR5's rated 150 N·m. Stretch out with 5 kg and watch the shoulder climb. The wrist reads zero because a gripper hanging straight down loads its axis with no lever arm.
